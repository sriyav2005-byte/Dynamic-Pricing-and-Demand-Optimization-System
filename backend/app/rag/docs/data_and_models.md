# Data provenance, models and their limits

## Synthetic data
The demo stores (Koramangala, Indiranagar) hold a synthetic (simulated) dataset: 160 FMCG / quick-commerce products across eight categories (fruits, vegetables, dairy, beverages, snacks, staples, household, personal care) with daily prices, stock and sales from 1 September 2025 to 30 September 2026.
Brand and product names are labels only; prices, costs, sales, stock levels and suppliers were generated by a simulation.
Everything derived from it must be labelled "Synthetic/Training Data". It is not real market or sales data.
Time windows in a synthetic store (for example "last 30 days") end at the last day of the dataset, not today.

## Competitor data labels
- LIVE — read from the competitor platform by PriceIQ within the last 6 hours, for the store's delivery location.
- MANUAL_VERIFIED — a price a staff member looked up on the platform and recorded, within the last 24 hours.
- CACHED — an older real observation (scraped or manual), or a search result served from the 30-minute cache.
- ESTIMATED — a modelled value. PriceIQ never produces estimated competitor prices.
- UNAVAILABLE — the platform could not be read and nothing was recorded; no price is shown.
Only observed prices (LIVE, MANUAL_VERIFIED, CACHED) are used for market averages and competitor alerts. Competitor prices are never fabricated, interpolated or guessed.
As of October 2026 Blinkit can be read through an ordinary headless browser for the store's location. Zepto and Swiggy Instamart answer automated requests with a bot challenge and BigBasket denies access; PriceIQ does not bypass such protections, so their prices must be entered manually (Record observed price) after checking the platform.
Competitor listings must be linked to a product by a user; PriceIQ does not auto-match listings because pack sizes differ.

## Seasonal considerations
A seasonal consideration is a planning assumption entered by a store manager on the Seasonal page: a season, festival, local event or weather spell, the products it applies to (one product, a category or the whole store), start and end dates, the expected demand change in percent and the supply condition (normal, surplus, limited, shortage).
Active considerations multiply the demand forecast used for price recommendations, simulations, forecasts and expiry markdowns. Category- and store-wide considerations are scaled by each product's own seasonal, festival or weather sensitivity (0 to 1).
A supply shortage stops discount recommendations for the affected products.
A consideration is always reported as a MANUAL assumption, never as a measured effect, and it cannot move a price outside the limits of the pricing constraint engine.

## Perishable products
Fruits, vegetables, dairy and other short-life products carry a shelf life, an expiry date for the batch in stock and seasonal / festival / weather sensitivities.
Wastage risk is the share of the stock on hand that is not expected to sell before expiry at the current price and sales velocity.
Pricing for perishables weighs demand, stock, days to expiry, expected waste (unsold units are counted as a loss), season, festivals, seasonal considerations and observed competitor prices.

## Demand model
- demand_xgb:v2 (the only model served): XGBoost with a Poisson objective, monotone decreasing price effects, and lag, calendar, festival and expiry features. It uses no product id, so it also serves new products.
- Validation is time-based: the last 30 days of the training data are held out. On the synthetic dataset the holdout error is about 5.6 units per day (R² about 0.73) versus about 5.9 for a naive 28-day average. Current figures are shown on the Settings page.
- A model trained on synthetic data has only learned the simulation. Retrain on a store's real history before relying on it.
- Forecast intervals (80%) come from a rolling-origin backtest. Accuracy figures shown with a forecast are out-of-sample.
- Weather is displayed for planning but is not a model input, because no historical weather is linked to the sales history. Use a weather consideration to reflect a forecast.
- The earlier v1 model (2023 dataset, product-id feature) has been retired.

## Retail Copilot
The copilot answers in one of two modes, and every reply states which one produced it.
- LLM mode: a hosted language model (reached through OpenRouter; configured on the AI service with AI_PROVIDER, OPENROUTER_API_KEY and AI_MODEL) decides which PriceIQ tools to call and writes the answer from their results.
- Rule-based mode: used when no language model is configured or the provider is unavailable, slow or rate-limited. It maps the question to the same tools without any language model.
In both modes every price, sales figure, stock level, forecast and competitor price comes from PriceIQ tools with the user's own permissions. The language model is not a source of business data, cannot run SQL and cannot change a price; it can only create a recommendation for a manager to review.

## Price elasticity
Estimated per product with a Poisson regression of units on log price, controlling for day of week, month and days to expiry.
Results are reported as ESTIMATED only when statistically significant (p < 0.05) and negative. With fewer than 30 days, fewer than 5 distinct prices or price variation under 2%, the status is INSUFFICIENT_DATA and no elasticity is claimed.

## Cross-product effects
Substitute / complement relationships are only reported when a cross-price effect survives Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery control (10%). When none is listed, no relationship is statistically supported.

## Pricing policies
- thompson_sampling_v2 (default): Thompson Sampling over 10 price arms with a prior from the demand model under the current context; updated only from real daily outcomes after deployment.
- contextual_ts_v1: linear Thompson Sampling, run in shadow mode for comparison until validated.
